# Methodology Checks

Methodology checks are diagnostic, not universal prescriptions.

## Research question fit

Ask:

- What is the target question?
- What quantity/phenomenon is being estimated, explained, described, interpreted, or explored?
- Does the stated design generate evidence relevant to that target?
- Are the inference claims stronger than the design permits?

## Quantitative checks

Review:

- population and sampling frame
- sample selection
- sample size rationale when required by the project
- operationalization
- measurement scale and construct fit
- missing-data handling
- exclusion rules
- model specification
- variable type compatibility
- assumptions and diagnostics when claimed/required
- robustness/sensitivity analysis where relevant

Do not declare a sample-size number “wrong” without knowing the design, effect assumptions, precision target, power framework, or project standard.

## Qualitative checks

Review:

- participant selection
- data source and collection process
- analytic approach
- coding/interpretive procedure
- evidence trail
- reflexivity/positionality when relevant to the methodology
- saturation or information-power claims only when the methodology actually uses them

Do not impose quantitative validity criteria on qualitative work.

## Mixed methods

Check:

- why both methods are needed
- sequencing/priority
- integration point
- whether the stated conclusions actually integrate the evidence

## Causal claims

Treat causal language as a claim-strength issue. Ask whether the design and assumptions identify a causal quantity. If context is missing, use `POTENTIAL METHODOLOGY CONCERN` and list what must be verified.

## Dead-end test

A research design is a potential dead end when the key research question requires evidence that the stated design cannot plausibly provide under the project's assumptions. Explain the mismatch and possible paths without forcing a redesign.
