# Evidence Checks

## Claim-evidence unit

Represent material claims as:

`CLAIM → EVIDENCE → INFERENCE → SCOPE`

For each important claim ask:

1. What exactly is claimed?
2. What evidence is cited or supplied?
3. What does the evidence actually establish?
4. Is the inference stronger than the evidence?
5. Does the evidence apply to the same population, period, context, construct, and method?

## Support classes

- SUPPORTED — evidence directly supports the stated claim.
- PARTIAL — evidence supports only part or a weaker version.
- UNSUPPORTED — no adequate evidence located in the supplied material.
- CONTRADICTED — supplied evidence points in another direction.
- INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE — available material is too incomplete to judge.

## Claim strength ladder

`DESCRIPTIVE < ASSOCIATIONAL < PREDICTIVE < CAUSAL < NORMATIVE`

Do not assume that a higher rung is invalid; verify whether the evidence/design justifies it.

## Citation checks

Check more than citation presence:

- cited source identity is traceable;
- source actually contains the claimed evidence;
- source population/context matches;
- cited result is not selectively strengthened;
- citation is attached to the correct sentence/claim.

Never invent a source to fill a gap.

## Novelty/gap claims

Do not call a gap globally novel without adequate literature coverage. Use cautious language such as `NOT ESTABLISHED FROM SUPPLIED EVIDENCE` when the literature set is insufficient.

## Result provenance and interpretation

For material empirical claims, distinguish the provenance/reproduction of a reported result from whether the claim's interpretation is supported. Trace the result through supplied data, transformations, analysis, output, thesis statement, discussion, and conclusion when those artifacts exist. Use the result-level statuses and matrix in `references/reproducibility-checks.md`.

A result may be reproducible while its interpretation remains unsupported or too strong. Conversely, missing inputs or blocked execution mean the result was not verified from supplied materials, not that it is false. Do not treat a reproduction limitation as evidence of misconduct.
