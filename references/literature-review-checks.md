# Literature Review Auditor

## Purpose
Audit a literature review as a synthesized research argument, not a stack of paper summaries. The goal is to detect weak synthesis, missing relevant evidence, unsupported gap claims, source-selection problems, and contribution inflation while preserving the review's intended scope.

## Audit dimensions

### 1. Review scope
Check whether the review states or implies a defensible scope: topic, population/context, period, study type, geography, language, and theoretical lens when material. A narrow scope is not automatically a flaw if it is justified.

### 2. Search and selection transparency
When a search strategy is supplied, inspect databases/sources, query terms, date range, inclusion/exclusion criteria, screening process, and duplicates. If no search strategy is supplied, do not invent one or automatically label the review incomplete; report what cannot be assessed.

### 3. Coverage
Check whether directly relevant literature is represented across the review's own scope. Look for obvious missing strands, major competing explanations, contradictory findings, and foundational/seminal work when central. Do not equate “not cited” with “does not exist.”

### 4. Synthesis quality
Prefer thematic, methodological, theoretical, or comparative synthesis over serial summaries. Check whether the review explains relationships among studies: agreement, contradiction, boundary conditions, measurement differences, methods, and unresolved questions.

### 5. Evidence balance
Check whether the review distinguishes seminal from recent sources, primary from secondary sources, and strong from weak evidence when that distinction matters. Avoid treating publication date alone as source quality.

### 6. Gap logic
Trace:
`Prior evidence → unresolved limitation → precise gap → research question/contribution`.
Flag a gap as weak when it is merely “few studies exist” without explaining why the missing work matters, or when cited literature already appears to address the claimed gap.

### 7. Cherry-picking and confirmation bias risk
Look for one-sided treatment of a disputed result, selective citation of supportive studies, or omission of clearly acknowledged counter-evidence. Phrase these as risk unless selection criteria and the full literature set make the conclusion stronger.

### 8. Literature-to-research alignment
Verify that the literature review actually motivates the hypotheses, constructs, methods, and contribution later used in the research. A literature section can be factually accurate yet still poorly connected to the study.

## Output
For each material issue, report: location, literature evidence, reasoning, impact on gap/RQ/hypothesis/contribution, confidence, and verification needed.

## Limits
Do not claim global completeness, novelty, or “all relevant studies” from a supplied citation list alone. External search may be used only when the environment and task explicitly support it; otherwise label coverage as bounded by the supplied literature.
